# Motivation

No new Nick Maggiulli overnight. Friday Forward #552, “Assembly Required,” is the piece that landed.

Glazer spent a 100-degree afternoon assembling an IKEA-style dresser for his daughter’s first New York apartment, then did it again a month later with his son in Philadelphia — Facebook Marketplace runs, two flights of stairs, a back that was already done. Friends said hire a TaskRabbit. He did not. Years earlier a successful executive had told a room to outsource everything that was not a core competency. Someone behind Glazer asked whether raising kids that way was a reliable way to produce entitled adults. The comment stuck.

The useful cut is the same test Maggiulli put on the MBA and Walsh put on the unpaid advisor: write down the counterfactual before you pay to skip the work. CEOs are not paid to book their own travel. A college student who DoorDashes every coffee because walking is inconvenient should at least be the one paying the bill, or the habit is a lifestyle they have neither earned nor can afford. On a team, strategy is the job — and occasionally washing the dishes after an event is how you show that no task is beneath the person who assigns them.

Just because you can afford to delegate it does not mean you should. Sometimes, for the people watching, that is exactly why you should not.
