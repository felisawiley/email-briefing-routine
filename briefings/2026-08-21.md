# Motivation

No new Nick Maggiulli or Friday Forward overnight. The piece worth sitting with is your own: the hiring essay that won the title test as “AI Won’t Kill LinkedIn. It Might Change Hiring Instead.”

The useful cut is not “LinkedIn dies.” Revenue is still growing, Talent Solutions is still a machine, and agentic hiring products were already a $450 million run-rate by April. The interesting problem is underneath the platform. Tailored résumés, personalized outreach, and high-volume applications used to leak a little information about effort. AI made those cheap. Personalization can now be accurate without being meaningful — a recruiter email that names your last post may never have been read by a person.

That is the same filter as Raines and Glazer this week, pointed at PausePoint instead of a book. Abundance was the product for twenty years. AI manufactures abundance. What becomes scarce is a reason to trust what is already in front of you: a body of work that existed before anyone was evaluating you, a community where reputation has consequences, a little useful friction that tells both sides something the other cannot fake. Do not write the how-to for getting cited. Write the thing that would still be true if nobody was hiring you today.
