# Motivation

The things you built at work become part of who you are, which is why handing them off feels like a loss instead of a promotion. Molly Graham’s “Give Away Your Legos” point is that people will not drop a project they own until a bigger pile is sitting in front of them. At Facebook she watched a colleague cling to a handoff until a manager put her on a growth push in India. The old set stopped mattering the moment a shinier one showed up.

That fear is expensive. Leaders who keep the old work spend their hours in the weeds and starve the organization of the next thing. The test is not whether someone does it exactly as you would. Robert Glazer’s 85 percent rule: if it gets done well enough without you, it is a win.

Leadership has two jobs: grow the people who can replace you, and eventually make yourself unnecessary. A team that falls apart when you step away was never a team. The question is which Legos you are still holding for identity, and what larger pile you could pick up if you let go.
