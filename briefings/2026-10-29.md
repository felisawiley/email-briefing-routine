# Motivation

The word "sociotechnical" keeps coming back, and that is the tell.

It was coined in 1951 because production engineers had optimized the machinery as if people were outside the system, and behavioral scientists had optimized morale as if the machinery were someone else's problem. The miners sat in the gap. The compound word was a patch, not a celebration of two fields finally meeting.

Every later surge — the 1970s design methods, the post-2008 systems literature, the current AI papers that insist models must be understood as sociotechnical — marks the same split opening in a new place. Specialization rewards problems that fit a discipline's language. Human context does not. So a subfield appears whose job is to put back what the core field shed in order to move faster. You do not staff a translation desk between people who still speak the same language.

The useful move is not a new label. It is staying accountable for a system's human effects after specialization has made those effects somebody else's problem. The knowledge was never missing. The practice that would have made the extra word unnecessary was.
