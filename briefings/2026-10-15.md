# Motivation

Jack Raines on Young Money: the problem with AI tools is not burnout from one long grind. It is the opposite. You can start ten projects from the couch, so you never have to choose. Friction used to force the trade. Writing a book took weekend blocks; twenty minutes on a Tuesday did not count. That constraint made “should” obvious.

Now “can” is cheap. Opening a coding assistant and talking a new idea into existence feels like work. It is closer to extra open tabs. You still only have one attention. The paradox of choice that wrecked jam aisles and dating apps now sits on your desk: infinite good options, almost no commitment.

Raines’s ask is to put the rocks in first. Instant answers skip the slow work that builds judgment. A summary is not the book. Token-maxxing feels like a priority because it looks like work; it is sand filling the cup. Decide what is worth doing before you ask a model to do it.
