# Motivation

Robert Glazer’s *Friday Forward* (#548) starts from a forced ending: aging out of a long-running YPO forum. In the final meeting, nearly everyone was in transition — businesses shifting, roles ending, kids leaving, relationships changing. The pattern he noticed was simple: every story about something exciting beginning started with something else ending.

Doors close two ways. When they close on you — a rule change, a lost client, a season that ends on someone else’s calendar — it stings, but unexpected openings often follow. Closing the door yourself is harder, because it means ending something that still works. Most people will leave a bad job or a broken partnership; fewer will trade “good enough” for the chance at great. The hidden cost of propping a comfortable door open for years is the room you never make for the next chapter.

Glazer’s own update mixed both: some doors closed on him, and those endings pushed him toward an opening he had been circling for a year. The room wasn’t lamenting. People describing endings were also excited about beginnings — not in conflict, but in sequence. The ask is narrow: if you’re holding a door open out of habit or fear, ask what could open once you’re willing to close it. Quote of the week: “Every new beginning comes from some other beginning’s end.”
