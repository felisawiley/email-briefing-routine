# Motivation

No new Nick Maggiulli overnight. Friday Forward #552 still stands. The piece that landed is Walsh’s Labor Day tape with Brett Turner.

Turner spent twelve years inside other people’s books — CPA, controller, CFO, including spectacular failures — before he founded Trovata. Most first-time founders do the reverse: ship the product, treat the numbers as a chore, hire a finance person the month the account hits zero. Walsh’s workbook is blunt: pull three months of bank data against the P&L and explain the gap in one sentence; name the three or four numbers that actually run the business; check the 83(b); look at the cap table like a stranger. Cash and profit tell different stories, and only one of them can bankrupt you this quarter.

That is Glazer’s dresser and Maggiulli’s MBA test pointed at the operating system. You can afford to outsource bookkeeping. You cannot afford to outsource knowing whether you are out of cash. Eighty percent of early startups have no CFO; the numbers sit with a founder who never trained for the role. Timing a hire wrong is expensive either way — but delegating the dashboard before you can recite it is how entitled companies, not just entitled adults, get made.

Cash tells the truth before the P&L does. Check it first.
