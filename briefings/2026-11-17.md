# Motivation

Robert Glazer went to see You Can See Everything, the Elizabeth Holmes documentary, because three people told him to. The film never hands you a verdict. One friend left thinking she was a founder who believed her own pitch. Another left thinking she was a liar. Both readings fit the same footage.

Holmes is tearful and adamant she did nothing wrong. That is easy to produce. What is harder to fake is the tape: tests run on ordinary lab machines, a cover-up, critics pushed aside. Glazer has changed his mind on her before — first that she might not have set out to deceive, later that narcissists are hard to see through because they feel no guilt. Emotion in the moment is the weakest evidence we get.

The useful part is not Theranos. Almost everyone has to deal with a compelling story that does not match the record. A self-deluded person can be sincere and still not be trustworthy. Weigh what they have done over what they say, even when the story is good. Seeing is one thing. Believing is another.
