# Motivation

Chemistry is not luck. Friday Forward’s astronaut story is three people who had almost nothing in common — a talkative Italian, an American who thinks out loud, a reserved Russian fighter pilot trained to shoot the other two down — sharing a tin can for six months. Early drills went badly. The fix was not a personality workshop. It was sitting down and saying why each of them wanted the job, then asking about the kid. They did not become best friends. They became a crew.

Similar tastes are a weak predictor of whether people click. Interaction is the predictor, and the fastest way in is the past. Almost every core value is a story from before this job. When someone tells that story, especially a leader, the team sees a person instead of a role.

Those conversations rarely happen in the stand-up. They happen at a meal, on a walk, after the work is put down. That is the point of the gathering that feels like a luxury: it is where trust gets built so the work can survive a hard week.

Not every pairing will ever feel easy. The useful question is whether you wrote someone off without learning the story. Compatibility is not finding the right person. It is working together until you find the wavelength.
