# Motivation

Robert Glazer’s monthly note is a reading list with a single spine: human connection is a skill, not a mood.

The line he cannot stop repeating is Aliza Pressman’s: all feelings are welcome; all behaviors are not. That is a parenting sentence that also works as a management one. You can make room for fear, frustration, and disagreement without treating every impulse as a plan. Lenore Skenazy’s version is the other half — people are usually more capable than the safety rules around them imply. Over-helping feels like care. It often trains dependence.

Adam Grant’s new book sits on the same shelf: chemistry with people is not luck. You can build it. Vanessa Van Edwards treats conversation the same way — the parts most of us dread have a playbook. The point is not to become warmer in the abstract. It is to stop waiting for connection to arrive and start practicing the small moves that make it.
