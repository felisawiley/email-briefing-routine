# Motivation

Nick Maggiulli’s Tuesday piece is about the life you would have lived anyway. At 27 he almost bought an MBA: two years gone and about $150,000 in tuition for a salary he was already close to. FREOPP’s Preston Cooper put numbers on that gut. MBA graduates’ median earnings at 45 sit at $88,000; the people who look like them without the degree sit at $83,000. Sixty-four percent of MBA programs have a negative return once you net out cost and lost years. High earnings are not a win if the counterfactual is also high.

The useful cut is who the credential is actually for. Students coming from lower-paying undergraduate majors get the lift. People already in large tech or a fast-growing startup, with equity doing the compounding, mostly do not. Nursing, computer science, law, and medicine often clear the bar. Humanities and education doctorates mostly do not. Maggiulli is careful: top-ranked programs are a different product than the median MBA, and AI may raise the premium if it hollows out the no-degree path. None of that is a verdict on you. It is the shape of the premium.

He skipped the degree and wrote once a week instead. The blog became the job. He names the survivorship. The test he wants you to keep is the same one Gordin’s funnel and Glazer’s “publish the revision” were pointing at: interest is the investment; the spreadsheet only prices the cost of that interest. If PausePoint or the Substack is the work, do not buy a credential to feel like you have permission. Write down the counterfactual — what you would earn, and what you would make, without it — before you pay for the title.
