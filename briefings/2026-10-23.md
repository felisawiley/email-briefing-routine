# Motivation

The people who sound most sure about AI are not always the people who understand it. Practice Field’s Katelyn Jones sits with a Shorenstein Center report that finds women more often answer “not sure” on opinion questions about AI — and pairs it with research that women are, on average, more accurately confident than men. They have a better sense of how sure they should be.

That is the useful posture right now. Nobody, including the labs, knows what this technology finally does to work, care, or judgment. Loud certainty fills the headlines anyway. Jones’s point is Adam Grant’s confident humility: enough faith to keep going, enough doubt to notice when you have the wrong problem.

For a data strategist, the practice is simple. When someone pitches an AI move, ask how sure they are and what they would need to see to change their mind. Widen who counts as an expert beyond the person who talks first. Curiosity is not a lack of leadership. It is how you stay accurate while the ground is still moving.
