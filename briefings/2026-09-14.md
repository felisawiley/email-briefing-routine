# Motivation

No new Nick Maggiulli over the weekend. Friday Forward #553 landed Friday: “Actual Coincidence.”

Glazer pulled a landline wire in the basement, then woke at 2 a.m. to an alarm fault. He pushed the wire back in. The beeping stopped. The next morning the technician said the alarm had been on cellular for years, the overnight fault was a lost cell signal, and the heroic reconnect had nothing to do with it. Glazer did not believe him until he pulled the landline out again and the alarm stayed quiet.

The only evidence he had was that the timing was too perfect to be random. He never needed another explanation, because the one he had fit. That is not evidence. Every coincidence feels too perfect; sometimes it just is. Conspiracies start the same way: a gap, a story that closes it, and no test. Real conspiracies exist. They get proven the same way the alarm theory died — by pulling the line out, not by assembling only the facts that fit.

That is Walsh’s empty feed and Maggiulli’s tax overlay pointed at a leadership habit. You can manufacture posts so the voice never has to be chosen, or manufacture losses so the basis never gets touched, or manufacture a causal story so the meeting can end. All three feel like production. Glazer’s question is the one to take into tomorrow’s agent training: what are you certain about that you have never pulled the lines out on? A satisfying explanation is not the same thing as a checked one.
