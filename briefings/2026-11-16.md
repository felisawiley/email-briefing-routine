# Motivation

Nick Maggiulli’s grandfather gambled away more than a million dollars at the track. The bigger loss was his family.

The same pattern is now in everyone’s pocket. After the 2018 Supreme Court ruling, legal sports betting exploded: U.S. sportsbook handle went from $6.6 billion to $165 billion in seven years, and more than half of men aged 18–49 now have an online sportsbook account. The house’s cut rose because parlays pay worse than the true odds. Prediction markets such as Kalshi and Polymarket then slipped the state rules sportsbooks still have to follow — lower age limits, no self-exclusion lists, no state gaming tax. The people most at risk, young men, are the ones the loophole reaches first.

Most people who bet treat it as entertainment with a known cost. Maggiulli’s point is the unknown cost. Legalization already tracks with more bankruptcies and more domestic violence after upset losses. His grandfather at least had to drive to the track. The bet now is a phone unlock.

Seeing the product is not the same as believing the pitch. The house is not confused about who it is built for.
