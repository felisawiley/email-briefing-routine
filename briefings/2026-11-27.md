# Motivation

Organizations used to buy the tool, go live, and spend a year figuring out what it was supposed to fix. Gabe at Organizational Gravity's point is that AI has inverted even that sloppy order. The shot goes out first. Ready and aim become optional.

The wreckage is familiar: pilots nobody uses and nobody turns off, running next to the old process, so the shop gets heavier. PwC's 2026 survey found more than half of leaders saying AI delivered neither higher revenue nor lower costs. McKinsey's State of AI has EBIT impact stuck at 37 percent. Budgets still move from people to licenses. The people who leave first are often the ones holding the place together — the human middleware who know which customer changes specs every Tuesday, none of which is in the process map the agent was trained on.

The time AI saves on the build is the chance to spend months naming the actual problem, then build. Most places will burn that time by compressing the timeline instead. A vague request is not a spec. An agent cannot invent one.
