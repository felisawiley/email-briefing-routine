# Motivation

We keep saying AI will give us more time for people. Gabe Rybicki's question is whether we will actually spend it that way.

The spare hours can become more work. They can become a thoughtful message that nobody had to sit with. Colette Molteni's point is sharper: before you remove effort, know which effort you are removing, and what it contributes. Some of it is compensating for a broken process. Some of it is the soup — the attention and judgment that make the work valuable, not just the object that comes out the other side.

Human middleware is not only rekeying data between systems. It is the trust that lets someone raise a concern, the explanation that helps another team understand a request, the manager who notices a person is struggling. A new system can make the visible process easier while leaving people responsible for every connection around it. That is not an efficiency gain until you count what they are still doing.

The practical test is simple. Before handing a task to a model, ask what the person doing it knows, who relies on their involvement, and what would have to change for the work to succeed without them. Then decide where the tool helps. A warm draft is useful. The relationship still needs someone in it.
