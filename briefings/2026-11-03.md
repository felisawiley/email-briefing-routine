# Motivation

Neither of them needed another project. Jessi Hempel sat Malcolm Gladwell and Kenya Barris down because they had made something anyway — a podcast — and the reason was almost embarrassing in its honesty: it was fun.

That is not how serious people are supposed to choose work. You wait for the gap, the credential, the raise. Gladwell already had the books. Barris already had the shows. What they wanted was a room where curiosity could go somewhere neither of them could go alone. Gladwell could not follow a conversation about Los Angeles bus routes until Barris was in it. The difference was the point.

Gladwell’s mother used to get asked what she and his English father could possibly have in common. She found the question puzzling. They shared a faith and the experience of growing up loved. The overlap was obvious to the people inside it.

The useful test is not whether a project is necessary. It is whether you would still do it if nobody was watching the outcome. Partnerships that last often start as play, then earn their weight because two people keep finding questions the other would not have asked.
