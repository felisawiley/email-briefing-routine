# Motivation

The consultant’s complaint is not that AI is dumb. It is that we keep asking it to do the thinking. She fed Claude real consumer-panel extracts with rules attached. The first pass sounded sharp and was myopic: it chased a few indexes, ignored large slices of the audience, and would have shipped a wrong persona if she had forwarded it. Two hours of arguing with the model produced the hypothesis that is now shaping a 2027 plan. The tell is not a hallucination. It is an answer that sounds finished.

The same sloppiness shows up as a tax on everyone else. Decks ten pages too long. RFPs that dump the thinking on the reader. Jay Friedman calls it the AI Foisting Tax: you saved twenty minutes and spent someone else’s hour. The fix is the same as it always was. What does this audience actually need to understand, and what can you cut.

AI is good at filling a blank wall. It does not have taste. The brands people remember are the ones that still spend on effort you can feel. The work the model cannot do for you is knowing enough to argue with it.
