# Motivation

Seattle Data Guy’s *The Problem With Asking Employees to “Take Ownership”* is a useful Monday reset for anyone building under the AI-era buzzword “agency.” Leaders say they want people who take initiative, then punish anyone who picks the wrong problem. The result is predictable: task-takers who wait to be told, and seniors who stop bringing ideas because the only feedback was bureaucracy.

True ownership is not freelancing inside the company, and it is not “never leave the sprint board.” It is independent execution inside a shared direction — finish the assigned work, earn trust, validate ideas before disappearing into a side quest, and tie proposals to business goals rather than “this would be fun to build.” If you manage people, you cannot ask for ownership while only rewarding obedience. If you are the builder, the growth path is still to propose — after you have delivered — and to treat course-correction as the cost of developing judgment, not as proof you should stop thinking.
