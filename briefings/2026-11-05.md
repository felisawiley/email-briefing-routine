# Motivation

Jonathan Wang’s poker lesson, the one Maggiulli flagged this week, is not about cards. A solver said raise half the hands. Two people at the table were already telling him what they would do — one by lifting cards to fold, one by playing almost nothing. The winning play was to raise every hand. The model was right for a table of models. The table was full of people.

Pay attention is the first half. The second is having the nerve to act on what you actually saw. Knowing the read and still playing the textbook line is the same as not looking.

Most human systems are not in equilibrium. That is usually where the opening is — a niche that looks occupied, a career habit that is only habit, time with people you keep postponing. Watch what people are doing, not what the playbook says they should do. Then go.
