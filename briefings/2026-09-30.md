# Motivation

Nick Maggiulli’s new piece on “Main Street Millionaires” is a reminder that the wealth you actually see — tech founders, hedge funds, the Forbes 400 — is not where most American riches sit. Economists Owen Zidar and Eric Zwick show that pass-through business owners hold more than thirteen times the Forbes 400. Law firms, auto dealers, consultants, local energy operators. Everywhere, not just the coasts.

The portrait is unglamorous: older, usually married, usually credentialed, and patient. The median age for decamillionaire owners is 62. Most firms were founded or bought, not inherited — though coming from money still raises the odds of founding. Your first job’s industry matters as much as the family you were born into: start in a place that produces entrepreneurs and you are more likely to become one.

The useful cut is the wealth-ladder one. A high savings rate and boring investing can get you to a few million. Getting past that usually requires ownership — starting or buying a business — and a lot of calendar time. Most founders never build a firm worth more than a few million, and many firms disappear within five years. Failure is common; destitution is not. You do not need that next rung to have a good life. If ownership is the path you want, treat it as a decades-long craft, not a lottery ticket.
