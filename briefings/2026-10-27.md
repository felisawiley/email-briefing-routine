# Motivation

Gabe’s “Twelve Forms of Human Middleware” names the unofficial job most organizations run on. Formal systems leave cracks. People fill them: they rekey data between tools, chase the next approval, remember why the exception exists, and keep two teams talking when the process map shows a clean handoff. The work is invisible because it succeeds. Then AI speeds everything up, and the same gaps become impossible to ignore.

He groups it three ways. Structural middleware is the seam work — technical, coordination, exceptions, governance. Behavioral middleware is the identity that forms around being the person who knows, or the person who rescues. Relational middleware is the trust graph process maps never draw. The useful question is not “can we automate this?” It is which of those loads we should remove, and which judgment we should protect.

For a data strategist, the trap is building a cleaner pipeline while leaving the human middleware unnamed. If a dashboard still needs someone to reconcile two sources, coach an exception, or carry context between teams, the system is not done. Name the load. Decide what to take off people. Leave the care that is actually the job.
