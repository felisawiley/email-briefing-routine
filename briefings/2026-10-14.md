# Motivation

Nick Maggiulli on the Fed’s 2025 Survey of Consumer Finances: from 2022 to 2025, median net worth for households 75 and older rose 38% after inflation, while households under 35 fell 23%. It is not simply “the rich got richer.” Young households lost ground across most of the wealth spectrum; older households gained even at lower percentiles. Similar net worth, opposite direction by age.

The mix of assets did the work. Homeownership among under-35s slipped from 38.5% to 35.6%, and the typical young homeowner is worth about 15 times a young renter — so a small drop in ownership pulls the median down. Older households also held more of their assets in stocks and retirement accounts through a roughly 48% real equity rally, while younger households held more in cars whose values fell.

Maggiulli’s plea is about timing, not guilt. Most inheritances arrive in the 50s and 60s, when the money matters less than it would at 30. If you have more than you will need, help now — a down payment, a few years of cheaper housing, the annual gift exclusion, or a job-market introduction. Young households are still richer than in 2019; the last three years look like a step back from a pandemic spike, not a collapse. The worry is the two wealth engines — a first job and a first house — getting harder to reach.
