# Motivation

When something gets cheaper, we do more of it — not less. That is Jevons Paradox, named for an economist who watched England burn more coal as steam engines got more efficient. Geoffrey Hinton once said we should stop training radiologists because AI would take the job. Instead, scans got faster, doctors ordered more of them, and radiology residencies and openings hit records.

The same pattern is showing up in software. Developer job postings rose, not vanished. AI was supposed to give time back. Almost nobody using it is doing less. Robert Glazer spent a sick weekend talking three websites into existence with Claude — sites that were not worth paying a designer for at the old cost. Without the cheaper tool, the work simply would not have happened.

Leaders who ask how to take a ten-person company to two are fighting human nature. The more useful question is how ten people use AI to do five times more. Not all of that extra output will be worth it. Cheap does not mean important. The paradox is not that jobs vanish. It is that cheaper capacity expands the list of things we try.
