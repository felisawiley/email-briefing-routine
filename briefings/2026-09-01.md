# Motivation

No new Nick Maggiulli or Friday Forward overnight. The piece worth sitting with is Monday’s Startups Decoded, Ep#84, with Vadim Gordin.

The useful cut is not the $2.7 billion his NYU studio helped 300 teams raise. It is the ratio he kept seeing and the founders who treated it like a character flaw. Twenty real conversations, one or two checks, every time, industry after industry. Warm intros convert at 8 to 12 percent. Cold email is 2 to 4. Median time to close a seed doubled to 142 days. About one in four companies that pitch angels get funded. None of that is a verdict on you. It is the shape of the funnel. Panic inside it and you run a marathon by accident. Trust the math and you can choose a sprint: lock the next meeting before you leave the first one, send traction on a clock, close a window on purpose.

The trap he names is skipping a rung. A stranger agrees to a conversation. Then they share information. Then a small pilot. Then they sign. Founders want the check before they have earned the meeting. The other trap is treating friends-and-family as a warmup. The habits you build raising the first dollars — how you track the twenty conversations, how you hold people who love you to a number and a date — are the same habits that will wreck or save the Series A cap table two years later. Write down the household guardrail before the raise puts real strain on the life.

That is last week’s Glazer note and Walsh’s “trust is the product,” pointed at PausePoint instead of Argentina. Belief is not a vibe. It is a stack of increasing commitments you can count. Twenty conversations is not failure. It is the work. Publish the conversion numbers the way you would publish a revision when a forecast breaks. Silence about the funnel is the expensive version of being right.
