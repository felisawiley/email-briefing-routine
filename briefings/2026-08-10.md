# Motivation

Adam Grant’s *The Truth About People Pleasing* reframes a habit many mistake for kindness. Chronic yes-saying is usually not about the other person’s feelings — it’s about securing their acceptance so your self-worth doesn’t wobble. Psychologists tie it to fear of rejection and insecure attachment: we learned that being cooperative to the point of deference kept us valued, or safe. The Uber-driver-AC example is small on purpose. Biting your tongue doesn’t protect you; it just trains the next person to freeze, and it trains you to put image above values.

Grant’s correction is useful: people pleasing isn’t “selfish,” but it is self-focused — we avoid making others uncomfortable because *we* can’t sit with their discomfort. Saying no isn’t disrespect. Sharing a preference isn’t aggression. Boundaries are how you stop outsourcing self-esteem to whoever is in front of you. If the weekend inbox is full of asks, the useful question isn’t “How do I stay liked?” — it’s “What would self-respect sound like in one clear sentence?”
