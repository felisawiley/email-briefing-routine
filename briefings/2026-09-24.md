# Motivation

Friday Forward #554, “Health Tracking.”

After a year and a half of feeling wrong and leaving every appointment with another referral, Robert Glazer started measuring sleep, strain, and recovery. The diagnosis never arrived. The pattern did.

What changed him was cause and effect, not a leaderboard. Sleep consistency mattered more than total hours. Heart-rate variability was an early warning that he was carrying too much. Days he pushed past recommended strain, he slept worse and recovered worse the next morning — the opposite of “work out hard so you’ll sleep.” Food within three hours of bed, and even one drink, showed up immediately. When cholesterol crept up, he found a compound in unfiltered coffee his doctor had never mentioned, switched to a paper filter, and it dropped.

The point is not to buy a wearable. It is to stop guessing. Pick two or three behaviors, watch what they do, and let the data argue with the story you tell yourself. Improving one percent a day is the unglamorous version of a New Year’s resolution — and the one that compounds.
