# Motivation

K.B. Johnson’s “In Defense of the Assisted Human” is about authorship, not speed. Cutting a thousand words from a journal manuscript used to take several anxious mornings. Now a one-line prompt proposes the cuts, she reviews them, and the coffee is still hot. The leftover unease is not the quality of the edit. It is whether using the tool counts as cheating.

That unease mismeasures the job. A colleague who suggests a better sentence does not become a co-author. An editor who rewrites an awkward passage does not either. The scientist is the person who decides what the work means, recognizes whether the words say it, and will defend the result. Measuring authorship by who typed the sentence is measuring the part that got easy to automate.

The useful split is augmentation versus vacation-mode delegation. Letting a tool play manuscript Tetris so you can think is one thing. Asking it to get the house ready while you leave town is another. Calculators and stats packages already changed how analysis gets done; “the computer did it” was never a defense. Difficulty is not evidence of intellectual value. Accountability still is.
