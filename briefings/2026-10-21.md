# Motivation

Nick Maggiulli’s new Survey of Consumer Finances cut is less a leaderboard than a translation guide. The average U.S. household is now worth about $1.23 million. The median is $216,000. Those two numbers describe different countries. Age makes the gap even sharper: median net worth runs from the low tens of thousands under 35 to about half a million after 75. Wealth climbs into the early sixties and then mostly plateaus. The 80-and-older median looks richest partly because the poorer households die first.

The number people always want him to strip out is the house. Do that and the typical household falls from $216,000 to $69,000. After 35, home equity is usually 55 to 70 percent of what a median family “has.” For the top fifth of an age group, it is closer to 15 to 25 percent. That is why the less wealthy you are, the more of your wealth lives in the building you sleep in — and why ignoring the house makes most people look poorer than they are. Home equity is still wealth. It is just not spendable wealth.

Comparison is useful because we otherwise guess from cars and vacations. It is not the point. The average is a carnival-mirror. Your goals are the only ranking that pays you back.
