# Motivation

Nick Maggiulli’s new post, “The Myth of Owning Your Time,” is a useful Tuesday correction if you are building PausePoint, consulting, or any “be your own boss” stack. Going out on your own does not delete the calendar. It just replaces a boss with clients, vendors, and the people who quit. The more successful you get, the more of you everyone wants — and “No” gets harder, not easier.

The data is blunt. High earners used to work *fewer* hours than low earners; by the early 2000s the top quintile was twice as likely to work long hours. An HBR study of large-company CEOs found 62.5-hour weeks and work on most weekends and vacation days. Maggiulli’s cut is the part that matters: having the option of time freedom and never using it is the same as not having it. Time does not transfer. Unused hours die with you.

So stop treating “own my calendar” as the prize. Decide what you actually want the hours to go to — family, a product, a practice — and give the hours up for that on purpose. Owning your time is a myth. Choosing how you don’t own it isn’t.
