# Motivation

Nick Maggiulli’s Tuesday piece, “Living Poor to Die Rich,” is the one that landed. Friday Forward #552 still stands.

The setup is a tax trick wealth managers love: a levered long/short overlay that keeps you in the market while manufacturing capital losses. On $1 million, Maggiulli says you can generate roughly 30% to 200% of that amount in losses over a decade, then use them to cut this year’s bill. Schwab has raised the minimum on some of these accounts to $10 million; Fidelity has paused new clients. The losses are the easy part. The trap is the matching unrealized gains you are now afraid to sell.

Stepped-up basis at death makes the future tax rate look like zero, so people take the positions to the grave. Maggiulli’s line is the one that sticks: money you will never spend is not really your money. An index fund may trap you at 70. The levered version can do it at 50, with decades of spending still ahead. Modest tax savings now, after fees and financing, in exchange for locking a huge chunk of capital until your children are in their fifties.

That is Glazer’s dresser and Walsh’s cash-flow tape pointed at the balance sheet. You can afford the strategy. The question is whether you can afford the life it buys — working, saving, watching it grow, and then not touching it. Live poor and die rich, or live rich and die a bit less rich. The money was for the second one.
