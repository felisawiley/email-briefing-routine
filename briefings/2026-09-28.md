# Motivation

Jess Ekstrom’s family lost everything in the Madoff fraud — and Madoff was family. When she visited him in prison, he didn’t start with a bad trade. He started with childhood: other kids wouldn’t hang around, so he paid them in nickels. That taught him attention could be bought. He never quit the habit.

Her test for whether a goal is actually yours: imagine you get the raise, the exit, the award — and you can never tell anyone. No post, no party, no dinner. Would the work still be worth it? If yes, go. If no, ask who you’re doing it for before you spend more years on it.

People who sell companies for life-changing money get a day or two of euphoria, then often go unhappy. Hit a goal, set a bigger one. Parenting has a public scoreboard — grades, sports, college bumper stickers — that vanishes when the kid leaves. What’s left is the time that didn’t need an audience.

Shift energy toward things you’d still do if nobody knew: values, close relationships, small present-tense joys. Madoff got the recognition he paid for. He died famous in a prison. Ask which items on this year’s list would still be worth doing if no one could know.
