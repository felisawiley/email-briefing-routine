# Motivation

The rent-versus-buy spreadsheet still says rent. Maggiulli’s own numbers: about $4,650 a month to rent versus about $10,000 all-in to buy, with 30-year rates above 7 percent. Two years after he decided to stay a renter, the math has not flipped.

The costs the spreadsheet misses get worse if you never buy. The nicest neighborhoods are often rental deserts — less than a fifth of the housing is for rent, and those blocks sit at much higher incomes. You also give up control: he has moved more than ten times in a decade. Coming out ahead takes discipline, because the renter has to invest the spread every month for decades, while a mortgage is forced saving you cannot easily dump in a panic.

The sharper point is that you will always need a place to live. Own roughly what you will consume, and a price drop hurts the asset but helps the next purchase. Own nothing while prices keep rising, and you get hit twice. Maggiulli’s call, with a family: he will buy within a few years even if rates stay high. Optionality won for a long time. Stability now tips the scale.
