# Motivation

Robert Glazer’s Friday Forward is a sideline lesson. When the Rams hired Sean McVay at 30, he kept drifting onto the field. The NFL does not allow that, so they assigned a strength coach to physically hold him back — a “get back coach.”

Most new managers never get one. They were good at the job they now supervise, so they step in the moment a report looks wrong. It feels like care. Over time the team waits for the rescue, and the leader becomes the bottleneck.

Glazer had to become his own get back coach on monthly client reports: teach the standard, let people miss it, then coach after. The work got better because it had to. Jump in when safety or a real loss is on the line. Otherwise ask whether you can explain the work without doing it, whether the team could get there with time, and whether your intervention will help next time.

For a data strategist, the equivalent is not rewriting the dashboard yourself. Leave the standard. Coach the miss. A great leader eventually learns how to operate from a distance.
