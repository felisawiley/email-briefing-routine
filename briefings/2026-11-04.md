# Motivation

The word millionaire still sounds like a private club. Nick Maggiulli’s new numbers say otherwise, and also not. About 29 million U.S. households — roughly one in five — now have a net worth over $1 million. That share has more than doubled since 1998. Then he takes the house and the 401(k) off the table, and the club shrinks to about 6 percent. Most of the people we call millionaires cannot write a check for a million dollars.

The rest of the story is age. The median millionaire household is 63. Under 35, it is 2 percent. Comparing a 40-year-old checking account to that group is like asking why a kindergartner cannot yet lift what a teenager can. Time in the market, and in a house, did a lot of the work.

Maggiulli’s point is not that $1 million is nothing. Globally it still puts you in the top 1.5 percent of adults. The useful move is to stop treating a rare, late-career, illiquid number as a report card for this morning. You can pick worse peers than that. You can also pick none.
